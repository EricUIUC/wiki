# General tips for school

# What do I do with all the credits I came in from High school?

Usually, the students who comes in with either a lot of credits or passed multiple proficiency exams has multiple different paths to take:
- Graduating early
- Taking a minor/2nd major or degree(check 3.Declaring a Minor for more details)
- Take less classes each semester
- Take other classes you are interested in

Here are the potential pros and cons of each available options:

Graduating early:
- Pros: can save lots of tuition fees, especially if you are an out-of-state or International student(especially if you start working early and assuming median salary via UIUC's own stats, you apprx save $30k and earn $50k per semester, which rounds out to an $80k opportunity cost per semester that you stay). Can also look impressive on your resume and open up time for you to go on coop experiences while still graduating in 4 years.
- Cons: extra stress depending on how much you want to cram your classes together, and it's very likely that you are taking 300-400 level classes within the first 1-2 years. You also lose out on potential college experiences you could've had in your senior year

Takin a minor/2nd major or degree:
- Pros: Allow you to widen your area of expertise within a more defined curriculum. Looks impressive on a resume too.
- Cons: potentially a lot of extra work depending on the minor/2nd major or degree's difficulty and how many classes you've actually knocked off via credits and proficiency exams

Take less classes each semester:
- Pros: Allow you to gain the full experience of college while saving time for you to do whatever you want(ie research, campus clubs, socializing, etc)
- Cons: without clear intent, the extra time can be easily wasted when you could be potentially skipping ahead

Take other classes you are interested:
- Pros: Similar to the less classes each semester option where you gain the full experience of college, except that you choose the time to take classes that can be potentially more defined than extracurricular options.
- Cons: Those extra classes does not show up on your resume, and you run the risk of not taking the opportunity to skip ahead as well.

Overall, the credits that are waived gives you a lot of different flexible options(some that may not even be listed!), and careful planning allows you to hone in on one of those options in order to push ahead. However, keep in mind that college is still a process of exploration, and keeping your mind flexible to potential opportunities that can arise while changing your plans accordingly will always trump sticking stubbornly to a plan that may no longer suit you.
